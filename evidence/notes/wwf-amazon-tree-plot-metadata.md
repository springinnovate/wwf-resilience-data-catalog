## Findings

Across 2,046 Amazon forest inventory plots, tree density, diversity and species richness varied strongly among soil–forest combinations and regions. Fisher's alpha diversity was highest in terra-firme forests of the western Pebas region and French Guiana, with lower values in white-sand, floodplain and swamp settings. Forest type explained 23% of variation in plot Fisher's alpha; a combined spatial model explained 66% for that untransformed metric. These results describe spatial variation, not trends through time.

## Using these data

The table contains plot-level density, richness, diversity and reference data underlying the regional models. Use it to distinguish those measures and examine the observations behind regional biodiversity maps. Conservation comparisons need to account for forest type and species composition as well as richness. Regional interpolations, species distributions and current deforestation require separate products and analyses.

## Limits

- Inventories concern undisturbed or old-growth forests and free-standing woody stems with diameter at breast height (DBH) of at least 10 cm. They do not cover every vegetation state or all life stages.
- All 2,046 supplied plots are retained, while individual modeling steps apply additional filters. Plot establishment year is not a measurement date or a repeated time series.
- Coordinate datum remains unverified. The source includes three establishment years in 2021 although the paper describes coverage through 2020. Species richness per hectare and at 500 trees are derived standardizations, not raw species totals.
