## Question

How much aboveground carbon is lost after different forms of tropical moist forest disturbance, and how do subsequent carbon stocks and accumulation differ between degraded forests and forests regrowing after clearance?

## What the study found

The meta-analysis synthesized 146 studies. Twenty years after the last disturbance, recovering degraded forests averaged (mean plus or minus one standard deviation) 75 ± 23% of reference aboveground carbon density (prior standing forest or nearby undisturbed forest), compared with 38 ± 16% for secondary regrowth forests. More intense and repeated disturbances were associated with greater losses. The paper reports faster accumulation as percentage points of reference stock during the first 20 years in secondary regrowth (+1.6 ± 0.8 percentage points per year) than in recovering degraded forest (+0.9 ± 0.7 percentage points per year).

## How to use the evidence

Disturbance history matters for carbon accounting and recovery expectations. Higher stocks in recovering degraded forest partly reflect carbon that survived the disturbance. The reported percentages compare remaining or accumulated stocks with a reference of prior standing forest or nearby undisturbed forest; they are not the fraction of previously lost carbon recovered. These are synthesis estimates rather than a controlled test of a specific conservation intervention.

## How the evidence was obtained

Literature synthesis and meta-analysis

## What this dataset contributes

Compiled study estimates underlying disturbance-loss and carbon-recovery comparisons.

## Limits and interpretation

- Studies differ in disturbance history, timing, spatial scale and measurement methods; the synthesis is more geographically complete in tropical America than in Africa, Asia and Oceania.
- The delivered workbooks contain 212 loss rows and 214 recovery rows, not one independent study per row. They are compiled inputs rather than new site observations or a spatial recovery forecast.
- RefID 96 labels two citations; four in-preparation loss records lack cached RefID values. Original formulas and cached values are retained without recalculation.
