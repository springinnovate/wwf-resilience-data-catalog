## Findings

The meta-analysis synthesized 146 studies of carbon loss and recovery in tropical moist forests. Twenty years after the last disturbance, recovering degraded forests averaged 75 ± 23% of reference aboveground carbon density, compared with 38 ± 16% for secondary forests regrowing after clearance (mean ± one standard deviation). The reference is prior standing forest or nearby undisturbed forest.

More intense and repeated disturbances were associated with greater losses. During the first 20 years, secondary regrowth accumulated carbon faster as percentage points of reference stock (+1.6 ± 0.8 percentage points per year) than recovering degraded forest (+0.9 ± 0.7 percentage points per year). Higher stocks in degraded forests partly reflect carbon that survived disturbance.

## Using these data

The workbooks contain compiled study estimates for comparing disturbance-related losses and subsequent carbon stocks. Use them to examine how disturbance history affects carbon accounting and recovery expectations. The reported percentages describe stocks relative to prior standing or nearby undisturbed forest; they are not the fraction of previously lost carbon recovered. These synthesis estimates are not a controlled test of a specific conservation intervention.

## Limits

- Studies differ in disturbance history, timing, spatial scale and measurement methods. Coverage is more geographically complete in tropical America than in Africa, Asia and Oceania.
- The workbooks contain 212 loss rows and 214 recovery rows, not one independent study per row. They are compiled inputs, not new site observations or a spatial recovery forecast.
- RefID 96 labels two citations; four in-preparation loss records lack cached RefID values. Original formulas and cached values are retained without recalculation.
