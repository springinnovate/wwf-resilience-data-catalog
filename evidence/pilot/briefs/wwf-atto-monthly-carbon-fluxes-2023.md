# ATTO forest carbon exchange during the 2023 Amazon drought

## Question

How did local forest carbon exchange at ATTO respond during the extreme 2023 drought?

## What the study found

The local eddy-covariance record remained a net sink in 2023. Reduced respiration contributed to the local balance, and the authors suggest sufficient soil moisture buffered the forest. Larger atmospheric footprints and basin-scale estimates showed a different response.

## How to use the evidence

The table supports examination of drought-response processes and model performance at one forest site. Climate-related carbon vulnerability is relevant to conservation planning, but this study did not evaluate a restoration treatment or policy at ATTO.

## How the evidence was obtained

Local flux observations interpreted alongside regional models

## What this dataset contributes

2023 monthly tower flux observations

## Limits and interpretation

- The prepared subset contains 12 months of 2023; anomaly calculations require the linked full 2014–2023 table and appropriate baseline handling.
- NEE is ecosystem respiration minus gross primary productivity; negative NEE denotes uptake. Units are grams of carbon per square metre per month.
- Local eddy-covariance and regional atmospheric concentration footprints differ. Do not apply the basin-wide carbon-source finding to this local flux table.
- Half-hourly observations and quality flags are absent. Estimates of respiration and productivity involve partitioning assumptions.
