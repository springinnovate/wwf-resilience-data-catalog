## Question

What spatial scale of wetland and river management is relevant to Amazon commercial fisheries?

## What the study found

The study reports that migratory species dominate known maximum commercial catches. Flooded forests help explain regional variation in catches of migratory characiform fishes, supporting a basin-scale management framework.

## How to use the evidence

Historical catch geography can help examine the dependence of fisheries on connected habitats and the need for cross-border coordination. Associations with wetlands do not measure the effect of a particular protection policy.

## How the evidence was obtained

Historical catch synthesis and observational spatial analysis

## What this dataset contributes

Mapped catch summaries underlying a management framework

## Limits and interpretation

- Catch values sum city-level historical annual maxima in tonnes per year; they are not a common-year census or current standing biomass.
- Contributing records span 1976–2013 with uneven coverage. Fishing effort, reporting and catchability affect observed catches.
- The authors use maximum catch as a proxy for known fish biomass; the delivered layer does not include the full wetland regression results or intervention estimates.
- The original rights text combines CC-BY-4.0 and share-alike wording; redistribution terms remain unresolved.
