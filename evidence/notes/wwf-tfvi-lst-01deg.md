## Findings

Statistical models of satellite and environmental observations found widespread vulnerability toward persistent land-surface warming across forests in the Americas. This pattern was associated with climate warming, atmospheric drying (increasing vapor-pressure deficit), and large-scale tree-cover loss. African forests generally showed lower sensitivity except where land-use pressures were extensive (Figure 5D).

## Using these data

The 0.1-degree Tropical Forest Vulnerability Index (TFVI) grid describes daytime land-surface temperature: the temperature of the land or vegetation surface. Positive values indicate vulnerability toward warming, negative values toward cooling, and near-zero values relative stability. A positive sign therefore does not mean ecological improvement. Compare the pattern with local temperature and forest-cover evidence to investigate pressures on forest cooling.

## Limits

- Values are dimensionless signed indices, not probabilities, measured changes or the physical units of the response variable.
- The model describes statistical relationships and assumes they remain stable. It does not estimate the causal effect of conservation or restoration.
- Figure 5 uses the separately supplied 0.25-degree grid; the primary file is the 0.1-degree version and the alternative remains linked.
- The exact observation interval of the grid is unresolved. Model uncertainty and significance were not recalculated.
