## Findings

Statistical models of satellite and environmental observations found widespread vulnerability toward lower gross primary productivity in the Amazon, with stronger negative signals along the arc of deforestation. Most forests in Africa and Asia showed lower vulnerability in this analysis (Figure 5B).

## Using these data

The 0.1-degree Tropical Forest Vulnerability Index (TFVI) grid describes gross primary productivity (GPP): carbon taken up through photosynthesis before respiration losses. Negative values indicate vulnerability toward decreases, positive values toward increases, and near-zero values relative stability under the analyzed stressor trends. Compare the map with forest-cover and climate observations to investigate where productivity may be under pressure.

## Limits

- Values are dimensionless signed indices, not probabilities, measured changes or the physical units of the response variable.
- The model describes statistical relationships and assumes they remain stable. It does not estimate the causal effect of conservation or restoration.
- Figure 5 uses the separately supplied 0.25-degree grid; the primary file is the 0.1-degree version and the alternative remains linked.
- The exact observation interval of the grid is unresolved. Model uncertainty and significance were not recalculated.
