## Question

Where are humid tropical forests vulnerable to changes in daytime land-surface temperature under climate stress and forest-cover change?

## What the study found

The paper reports widespread vulnerability toward persistent land-surface warming across forests in the Americas. It associates this pattern with warming climate, increasing vapor-pressure deficit, and large-scale tree-cover loss; African forests generally showed lower sensitivity except where land-use pressures were extensive (Figure 5D). This is a reported study result, not a temperature-change estimate derived anew from this file.

## How to use the evidence

Positive index values indicate vulnerability toward higher land-surface temperature, so a positive sign means warming rather than ecological improvement. Negative values indicate the opposite direction and near-zero values relative stability. Use the map as diagnostic context for forest energy-balance pressures. It neither supplies temperatures in degrees Celsius nor predicts the outcome of a restoration intervention.

## How the evidence was obtained

Diagnostic model using satellite and environmental observations

## What this dataset contributes

0.1-degree TFVI diagnostic grid for daytime land-surface temperature.

## Limits and interpretation

- The model describes statistical relationships and assumes they remain stable; it is not a causal evaluation of conservation or restoration.
- Values are dimensionless signed indices, not probabilities, measured changes, or the physical units of the response variable.
- Figure 5 uses the separately supplied 0.25-degree grid; the primary asset is the 0.1-degree version, with the alternative retained as a linked file.
- Model uncertainty and significance were not recalculated. The grid does not contain a verified observation interval.
