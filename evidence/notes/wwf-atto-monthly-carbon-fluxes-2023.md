## Findings

Local eddy-covariance measurements near the Amazon Tall Tower Observatory (ATTO) showed that the forest remained a net carbon sink over 2023 despite the extreme drought. Reduced respiration contributed to the local carbon balance. The authors suggest that sufficient soil moisture buffered the forest. Regional atmospheric measurements and basin-scale estimates showed a different response.

## Using these data

The table contains 12 monthly carbon-flux totals for 2023. Use it to examine local drought-response processes and ecosystem model performance. Anomaly calculations require the linked 2014–2023 table, which lacks May 2021; select and document the comparison years. Net ecosystem exchange (NEE) is ecosystem respiration minus gross primary productivity; negative NEE denotes uptake. Units are grams of carbon per square metre per month.

## Limits

- Local eddy-covariance and regional atmospheric concentration footprints differ. The study's basin-wide carbon-source finding does not describe this local flux table.
- Half-hourly observations and quality flags are absent. Estimates of respiration and productivity involve partitioning assumptions.
- The study did not evaluate a restoration treatment or policy at ATTO.
