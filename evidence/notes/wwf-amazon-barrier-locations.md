## Findings

The study combined fish-distribution and river-connectivity models to examine access to suitable habitat under climate change and hydropower expansion. It identified extensive connected, suitable habitat along the Amazon–Solimões corridor and projected contraction as climate and barriers changed. These results come from the combined models, not from the barrier points alone.

## Using these data

The layer contains the barrier locations used as model inputs: 562 points, comprising 359 dams and 203 waterfalls. Use it to examine where human-made and natural barriers enter the river-connectivity scenarios relevant to energy development and river conservation. The future network combines current and additional future barriers; the 269 future-tagged records alone do not form the complete future network.

## Limits

- Natural waterfalls and dams have distinct ecological and management interpretations.
- Connectivity indices, species-suitability results and impact rankings are absent. Assessing or ranking an individual dam requires the study's connectivity and species-model outputs.
- Observation and dam commissioning dates are unresolved; catalog time is a source modification date.
