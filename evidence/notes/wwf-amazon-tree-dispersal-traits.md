## Question

How widespread is animal-mediated seed dispersal among Amazonian trees, and how do seed and fruit traits differ among dispersal modes?

## What the study found

The paper’s Discussion summarizes 78–83% of the sampled species as animal dispersed, depending on classification, with arboreal vertebrates prominent. Animal-dispersed species generally have fleshy fruits. The analysis identifies a small set of abundant genera that contribute a disproportionate share of estimated fruit resources. The delivered table provides the underlying dispersal classifications and seed traits for 5,201 species; it does not include all pollination fields or population-weighted network calculations.

## How to use the evidence

These trait assignments describe potential dependencies between trees and dispersers and help identify why animal loss could matter for regeneration. They do not measure changes in recruitment after animal loss or estimate the effect of a restoration intervention. Claims about combined pollination and dispersal dependence come from the broader study and cannot be reproduced from this selected dispersal table alone.

## How the evidence was obtained

Species-trait and dispersal-mode compilation

## What this dataset contributes

Selected species-level dispersal assignments and seed traits from the broader network dataset.

## Limits and interpretation

- The two classification systems use different categories and some species have multiple assignments; they should not be added together as independent species counts.
- Dispersal assignments draw on literature and taxonomic inference, including genus-based assignments where appropriate; they are not all direct observations of interactions for each species.
- The paper gives internally inconsistent exact counts/percentages in its dispersal Results; the summary uses its Discussion’s approximate range rather than silently resolving them.
- Seed-mass values are logarithmic class codes, with exact class boundaries unresolved in the delivered metadata. The selected table alone does not reproduce pollination-network or population-weighted conclusions.
