# Forest productivity vulnerability to climate and forest loss

## Question

Where are humid tropical forests vulnerable to changes in gross primary productivity under climate stress and forest-cover change?

## What the study found

The paper reports widespread vulnerability toward reduced gross primary productivity in the Amazon, with stronger negative signals along the arc of deforestation. Most forests in Africa and Asia had lower GPP vulnerability in this analysis (Figure 5B). These are findings from the study analysis, not a new statistical analysis of the delivered grid.

## How to use the evidence

Use this diagnostic map to examine where forest productivity may be vulnerable under the stressor trends analyzed in the paper. Negative values indicate vulnerability toward decreasing GPP, positive values toward increasing GPP, and near-zero values relative stability. Values are standardized and dimensionless, not measured carbon uptake or probabilities. Use in conservation planning requires other evidence; the study did not evaluate a restoration treatment.

## How the evidence was obtained

Diagnostic model using satellite and environmental observations

## What this dataset contributes

0.1-degree TFVI diagnostic grid for gross primary productivity.

## Limits and interpretation

- The model describes statistical relationships and assumes they remain stable; it is not a causal evaluation of conservation or restoration.
- Values are dimensionless signed indices, not probabilities, measured changes, or the physical units of the response variable.
- Figure 5 uses the separately supplied 0.25-degree grid; the primary asset is the 0.1-degree version, with the alternative retained as a linked file.
- Model uncertainty and significance were not recalculated. The grid does not contain a verified observation interval.
