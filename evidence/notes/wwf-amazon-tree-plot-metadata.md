## Question

How do tree density, diversity and species richness vary across Amazonian forest types and regions, and what observations support the mapped patterns?

## What the study found

The study used 2,046 inventory plots and found strong variation among soil–forest combinations and regions. For example, Fisher’s alpha diversity was highest in terra-firme forests of the western Pebas region and French Guiana, with lower values in white-sand, floodplain and swamp settings. Forest type explained 23% of variation in plot Fisher’s alpha, while a combined spatial model explained 66% for that untransformed metric. These are descriptions and models of spatial variation rather than trends through time.

## How to use the evidence

Plot measurements help distinguish density, richness and diversity and test the basis of regional biodiversity maps. Conservation comparisons require attention to forest type and species composition, not richness alone. The delivered table contains observations and derived plot metrics; regional interpolation, species distributions and current deforestation are separate products and analyses.

## How the evidence was obtained

Forest inventory observations with spatial modeling in the associated study

## What this dataset contributes

Plot-level density, richness, diversity and reference data underlying regional models.

## Limits and interpretation

- The observations concern undisturbed or old-growth forest inventories and stems with DBH at least 10 cm; they do not represent every vegetation state or all life stages.
- All 2,046 supplied plots are retained, while individual modeling steps apply additional filters. Plot establishment year is not a measurement date or a repeated time series.
- Coordinate datum remains unverified. The source includes three establishment years in 2021 although the paper describes coverage through 2020. Species richness per hectare and at 500 trees are derived standardizations, not raw species totals.
