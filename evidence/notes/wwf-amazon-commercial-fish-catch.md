## Findings

Migratory fishes accounted for more than 80% of known maximum commercial catches in the Amazon. In the study's observational spatial analysis, flooded forest area helped explain regional variation in catches of migratory characiform fishes. The authors proposed managing fisheries and wetlands across river basins and national borders to account for these habitat connections.

## Using these data

The layer maps catch summaries for 20 commercial fish taxa across 198 sub-basins and mainstem sections. Values are sums of city-level historical annual maxima, in tonnes per year. Use the layer to examine the geography of those catches and their relationship to connected river and wetland habitats. The full wetland regressions and their results require the study's additional analyses.

## Limits

- The maxima come from different cities and years within 1976–2013, with uneven coverage. They are not a common-year census or current standing biomass.
- Fishing effort, reporting and catchability affect observed catches. The authors use maximum catch as a proxy for known fish biomass; these associations do not measure the effect of a protection policy.
- The original rights text combines CC-BY-4.0 and share-alike wording; redistribution terms remain unresolved.
